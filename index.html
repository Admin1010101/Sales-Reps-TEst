Ovena Health · Appointment Setter Training

Your job: find practices that place skin grafts, reach the right person, and book a qualified Lunch & Learn.

1. Your job

10 minutes · Read it twice

You book 15-minute lunches (Lunch & Learns) between our sales rep and practices that already apply skin grafts. You do not sell. You do not quote prices. You do not answer clinical or insurance questions. You find the right person, ask a few questions, and put a meeting on the calendar.

A win, in order

  1. Booked lunch with a practice that does grafts, clinician confirmed to attend.
  2. Booked call (15 min) with the decision-maker when lunch is not possible.
  3. Agreed follow-up: named person, topic, email, and a date you will call back.
A "send me info" with no name, email, or callback date is not a win. Get all three or it does not count.

Daily targets

  • 50 dials minimum.
  • Every call logged the same day.
  • Every agreed email sent the same business day.

Read the scripts out loud five times before your first call. Say the words exactly as written until they feel natural, then make them your own.

2. What we sell, in plain English

20 minutes

Ovena Health is a US-based wound care supplier. Practices buy from us directly, not through a middleman. You need two products, one sentence each.

Skin grafts (your main focus)

When a wound will not close on its own (diabetic foot ulcer, venous leg ulcer, pressure wound), the clinician can place a graft over it. A graft is a thin sheet of medical tissue that gives the wound a scaffold to heal on. We carry over 20 graft options covering conservative wound care (standard, lower-intensity treatment) and advanced wound care (hard-to-heal wounds). You never name a product.

Your line"We carry over 20 skin grafts for conservative and advanced wound care, and our team walks your clinicians through which ones fit your patients and your payers."
If they go deeper (which graft, sizes, Medicare, coverage, how to apply)"That is exactly what the lunch is for. Our specialist covers that in person."

Compression socks (second product)

Medical-grade compression socks for patients with swelling, poor circulation, lymphedema, vein problems, or pregnancy. The practice partners with us: we check whether each patient's insurance covers the socks. Covered goes through insurance; not covered, the patient pays out of pocket at a price well under retail. The practice carries no inventory and does no billing.

Your line"We set up a compression sock partnership. Your office refers the patient, we check their coverage and ship the socks. No inventory, no billing on your side."

Everything else

Collagen dressings, kits, saline wash, hydrocolloid, sock aids. You may hear these words. Write them down and move on. The rep handles it.

Three roles

  • Gatekeeper: front desk. Protects the doctor's time. Be short, friendly, specific. Never pitch them.
  • Decision-maker: office manager, practice administrator, clinic manager. Books the lunch. This is who you want.
  • Clinician: the doctor, NP, or wound nurse who places grafts. Must be at the lunch. You rarely talk to them directly.
The one question that qualifies a graft lead: "Does your team apply skin grafts in the office?" No, and no plans to start, means socks or a polite close.

4. Get past the front desk

20 minutes

Front desks route calls two ways. A stranger asking "who handles ordering" goes to voicemail. Someone asking for Dr. Patel by name, with a reason, gets put through. Spend five minutes before each call so you are the second kind.

Before you dial

  • Practice name, city, state, specialty confirmed.
  • Call log open. Rep's calendar open with two lunch slots to offer.
  • Approved callback number and sending email ready.
  • Call 9:30 to 11:30 am or 1:30 to 4:00 pm in their time zone. Avoid Monday mornings and Friday afternoons.

Five-minute research, every practice

  1. Practice website. Providers / Our Team page: write down every doctor, NP, PA with title. Services page: look for "wound care," "skin substitutes," "grafts," "advanced wound care," "diabetic foot," "limb salvage." Any of those means they place grafts. Copy the exact phrase.
  2. NPI Registry (npiregistry.cms.hhs.gov). Search the practice or doctor. Confirms full name, specialty, address. 60 seconds.
  3. Google reviews. Scan for "wound," "ulcer," "graft," "healed." Patients name the doctor. Note who signs the practice's replies (often the office manager).
  4. LinkedIn. Search the practice name. Office managers and practice administrators list it. Get the name.

Write it on the lead before you dial: clinician name and title, office manager name if found, exact wound care wording from their site.

The assumptive opener (use it whenever you have a name)

"Hi, is Dr. [last name] in the office today?"
Say it like a colleague, not a salesperson. Then stop.
"Yes, who's calling?"
"[Your name] with Ovena Health. I'm reaching out about the skin grafts for her wound care patients. Is she between patients, or is [office manager] the better person for scheduling?"
"She's with patients."
"Figured. Who handles her schedule for vendor meetings? I just need two minutes with them."
"What is this regarding?"
"Grafts for the practice's wound care patients. Your site mentions advanced wound care, and we carry over 20 grafts for that. I'm trying to find 15 minutes for Dr. [last name] and the team."
"Is she expecting your call?"
Only say yes if it is true. Make it true: send the graft email the day before, then open with the follow-up line below.
Never claim a relationship you don't have. Never say "we've worked with the practice before" or "the doctor asked me to call" unless it happened. Sound familiar by using their name, their website's words, and a specific reason.

Email first, call second

For top-priority practices (website says grafts or advanced wound care), send the graft email first, then call the next business day.

"Hi, this is [your name] with Ovena Health following up on an email I sent [name] yesterday about skin grafts for your wound care patients. Is [name] available?"

You are now a known contact, not a cold call. Front desks put follow-up calls through.

8. Qualified lead gate and the two emails

20 minutes

No free lunches: all five or it's a call

A lunch costs the rep half a day and the company money. Before you offer one, you must have all five written on the lead. Missing one means you offer a 15-minute call instead.

  1. They place grafts now. Said yes to "Does your team apply skin grafts in the office?" Not "thinking about it." Not "we refer out."
  2. Volume. A number or range per month. "A handful" counts. "I don't know" does not.
  3. Clinician named and attending. The graft-placing doctor, NP, or PA is named and confirmed in the room.
  4. Current product or supplier named. Proves they actually buy grafts.
  5. Payer mix. Mostly Medicare, mostly commercial, or a mix.

Red flags: downgrade to a call or walk away

  • Office manager wants the lunch but can't name the clinician or won't commit them.
  • They ask about the lunch before they ask about the products.
  • "We love lunch and learns" with no answer on volume or supplier.
  • Website shows no wound care, podiatry, or vascular, and they can't describe graft use.
  • They want to book three months out. Real interest books within three weeks.
When it smells like free food"Before I put our team on the road, I want to make sure it's worth [clinician]'s time too. Can you confirm they'll be there and roughly how many graft patients they're seeing?"
If the answer is vague"Let's start with a 15-minute call with [clinician] and our specialist instead. If it's a fit, we'll set up the lunch from there."

Day-before confirmation call

"Hi [name], confirming tomorrow's 12:00 lunch with Dr. [name] and our team. Is Dr. [name] still able to join?"
Clinician dropped out? Reschedule. Do not send the team to feed the front desk.

Two emails. Pick by product. Same business day. Under 90 words. One attachment, two at most. Replace every bracket. Never attach pricing, coverage, or clinical documents; the specialist sends those after the meeting.

Follow-up timing

  • Booked: invite within the hour, confirmation call the business day before.
  • Follow-up agreed: call on the date they gave you. Not before.
  • No date agreed: one call three business days later, one more a week after. Then the 60-day list.

Log every call, same day

Practice, contact and role, state, setting, clinician name, grafts yes/no, volume, current supplier, payer mix, what you sent, follow-up permission, next action and date, meeting details. No patient information, ever.

Never say

  • Any price, discount, or reimbursement number.
  • "Medicare covers it" or "your insurance will cover it."
  • Any specific graft name. "Over 20 grafts" is the whole answer.
  • "It heals wounds faster" or any healing claim.
  • "We can send samples."
  • "We've worked with your practice" or "the doctor asked me to call" unless it happened.
  • Anything negative about a competitor or the practice's current process.
  • Where the company is based. You are a US-based supplier. That is enough.
  • "Can I ask you a couple of quick questions?" Just ask the question.
