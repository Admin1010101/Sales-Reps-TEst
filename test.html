Money Math — what a deal pays you

First read the 3 rules. Then build a pretend order below and watch the numbers change.

The 3 rules — that's all there is
  • Rule 1 — Buy more, pay less. Customers get a discount for bigger orders: 10+ items = 10% off · 25+ = 15% off · 50+ = 20% off. That's the whole discount system. Nothing deeper without Harold's OK — never make up a discount on the phone.
  • Rule 2 — You get a cut of every order. Your cut is 12% of what the customer pays. And when an order hits 50+ items, your cut jumps to 15%. So big orders pay you twice: bigger total AND a bigger percent.
  • Rule 3 — Tiny orders pay you 5%. We WILL sell someone 1 or 2 boxes — but your cut drops to 5% on those. So don't chase them. Your job is bulk orders (10+) that repeat every month.

What we sell — 3 things now, 1 thing next month

Keep it simple. Right now your bag has exactly three offers in it.

✓ Selling now

1 · Collagen by itself

Just the healing layer. 5-packs: 2×2 ($32.99), 4×4 ($55), powder ($67.99). For offices that already have their own bandages and tape — they just need the collagen. The powder is our special weapon: it works on deep, odd-shaped wounds that a flat pad can't touch.

The collagen money story: collagen is insurance-reimbursable. Offices that bill can use the A-codes: a 2×2 or 4×4 piece pays back about $19–$25, and powder about $40 per gram. We don't touch the billing — THEY bill, THEY collect. We just show them what they can make. (The "we try insurance for you" offer is for SOCKS only — never say it about collagen.)

✓ Selling now

2 · Collagen kits

Everything for one dressing change in one box (2×2 kit $36.99 · 4×4 kit $59.99 · Wound Kit in 5-set/10-set). And a card to keep in your back pocket for emergencies only: a buyer can pay 50% now and the rest in 30 days. Never lead with it — use it only when money is the LAST thing stopping a yes.

✓ Selling now

3 · Compression sock partnership

The doctor sends patients our way (or sells socks right in the office). We try the patient's insurance first. If insurance says no, the patient pays our partner price: $24 a pair + shipping — instead of $40–$60 at the store. Either way, the patient actually gets their socks. And you manage the account.

⏳ Coming in about 30 days — do NOT sell it yet

Silicone foam dressing kit (by itself)

Our own product, priced to beat the big guys. For now, just plant the seed on calls: "Next month I'll have something new for your everyday dressings — I'll bring it to you first." That gives every customer a reason to pick up your NEXT call.

One rule to never break: never promise that insurance will cover something. The honest line — "we try insurance first; if it's not covered, your patients still get our $24 price" — closes more deals than a promise you can't keep.

Who we call — 7 types of customers

Every card has 4 parts: what they WANT, what HURTS them, what you SELL them, and what you SAY first. The pain part is the most important — you sell by fixing pain.

🦶 Podiatrists (foot doctors)

Best all-around customerCollagen ✓✓Kits ✓Socks ✓✓
What they want

Treat feet well — and make extra money doing it.

What hurts them

Their wound patients get sent to a pharmacy or Amazon for supplies — so the doctor makes $0 on any of it. Patients half the time never even buy the supplies, come back worse, and the doctor gets blamed.

What you sell

The MAKE-money story, two ways. Cash: they buy our kits and socks at wholesale, sell them in the office at their price, keep the profit. Insurance: offices that bill can use the collagen A-codes — a 2×2 or 4×4 piece reimburses about $19–$25, powder about $40 a gram. We don't do the billing; we just show the math.

Say first

"How do your wound care patients get their supplies today — do they leave with them, or get sent somewhere?"

🩺 Lymphedema, vein & vascular doctors

The #1 target for socksSocks ✓✓Collagen ○
What they want

Their patients to actually WEAR compression — for swelling (lymphedema), after vein surgery, and for circulation problems. Compression IS the treatment.

What hurts them

They tell a patient "you need compression socks" — then the patient sees $40–$60 at the store, insurance often says no, and the patient just... skips it. Swelling comes back. Surgery results get worse. The doctor looks bad and can't fix it.

What you sell

The sock partnership: we try insurance first; if it's a no, their patients pay $24 a pair. Suddenly patients actually buy them and actually wear them. Some offices also stock and sell them directly (more MAKE-money). Sock aids for older patients who can't pull them on.

Say first

"When you prescribe compression, how many of your patients actually go buy it?" (They know the answer is 'not enough.' That's the whole call.)

🏠 Home health agencies

Kits ✓✓NEVER lead with collagenSocks ○
What they want

Keep patients stable AT HOME with fewer visits. They are NOT trying to heal wounds fast — that's not their job or their goal.

What hurts them

Nurses show up to a patient's living room and something's missing from the bag. Someone in the office spends half their week ordering from 3 different suppliers. Every nurse's bag is packed differently. Margins per visit are razor thin, and supply chaos eats them alive.

What you sell

The SAVE-money story: one Wound Care Kit box = a complete dressing change. Every nurse carries the same box. Nothing's ever missing. One order, one invoice, reorders run themselves — you manage it. Collagen only comes up later, when a wound stalls.

Say first

"How much of your week goes to supply chases and missing items in the field?"

🛏️ SNFs (skilled nursing facilities)

Collagen ✓✓Kits ✓Socks ○
What they want

To actually HEAL wounds. State inspectors grade them on it — the opposite of home health.

What hurts them

A wound that won't close is a black mark on their inspection, and can even mean fines or a lawsuit. Stalled wounds are their nightmare.

What you sell

The healing story: collagen restarts stalled wounds, powder handles the deep ones nothing else fits. Kits keep every change consistent. Talk cost per dressing change, not cost per box.

Say first

"How many wounds in the building right now have been stuck for over a month?"

🚗 Mobile wound care

Kits ✓✓Collagen ✓
What they want

Fast, smooth visits — they treat patients out of a car all day.

What hurts them

Getting to a bedside and realizing the tape or wash is back at the office. Restocking the car is chaos. Time lost = visits lost = money lost.

What you sell

One kit = one visit, grab and go. Nothing missing, ever. Auto-reorder so the trunk stays stocked. These buyers feel the 5-things-from-5-places pain the hardest — this is your easiest consolidation sell.

Say first

"What happens when you're at a bedside and something's missing from the car?"

🏥 Wound care clinics

Collagen ✓✓Kits ✓Socks ✓
What they want

Depends on the clinic. Well-funded clinics want great results and a premium patient experience. Tight-budget clinics want to stretch every dollar.

What hurts them

Both types juggle several suppliers plus "Amazon when we run out" (which means stockouts AND paying retail). Tight-budget clinics also have patients who can't afford supplies at all — so patients skip care.

What you sell

Well-funded: premium US-based products + everything in one order. Tight-budget: best total price, $24 socks their patients can actually afford — and if cash is the ONLY blocker, the back-pocket split terms (50% now, rest in 30 days). Ask which kind you're talking to — then pick the story.

Say first

"How do you buy supplies today — one supplier, or a bit of everywhere?"

The sorting question — memorize this one: "What's the goal for these patients — healing them fast, or keeping them stable and comfortable?"

If they say healing (SNFs, podiatry, wound clinics) → tell the collagen story. If they say stable (home health) → tell the kit story. If they can sell to patients (podiatry, vein/vascular) → add the MAKE-money story on top. That one question routes every call.

The questions — how you start every call

You are not calling to pitch. You're calling to find out what's broken. Ask, listen, and they will tell you exactly what to sell them.

Click a card to flip it → say the question OUT LOUD before reading the back.

The listening rules: talk 30%, listen 70%. Write down every supplier name and every price they mention — that's ammo for your next call. Every complaint they say out loud IS your pitch — just repeat it back with our fix. And end every good call with: "Who else do you know that should hear this?"
